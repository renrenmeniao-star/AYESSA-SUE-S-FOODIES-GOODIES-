History of the Business

The journey of Ayessa Sue’s Foodie’s & Goodies is a story of passion, resilience, and unwavering faith in the power of good food. Long before the business had a name, Ayessa carried a deep love for cooking and entrepreneurship. Even before she became a teacher, she believed one simple truth: people will always look for food that brings comfort, joy, and connection. Despite experiencing several business failures, she held on to her dreams — knowing that every setback was shaping her for something bigger.

In April 2020, at the height of the pandemic lockdown, life slowed down and days became quiet. But from that stillness came a spark. Out of boredom and determination, Ayessa began selling snacks online. A week later, she added home-cooked meals, and in no time, orders poured in. Deliveries stretched from Argao to Oslob, proving that her flavors spoke for themselves.

By July 2020, she transformed a small corner of her garage into a modest food display. It wasn’t grand, but it was the beginning of something meaningful. Then in 2021, a new chapter opened when her sister and brother-in-law acquired the former Uniway Bibles Church. With perfect timing and renewed courage, Ayessa opened her first physical store on October 18, 2021.

But just when everything seemed to be falling into place, Typhoon Odette struck on December 16, 2021. In a single night, the business was washed out — stocks gone, equipment destroyed, and the heart of her store left in ruins. It was a moment that could have ended everything. Instead, it became the moment that strengthened her the most.

A few weeks after the typhoon, Ayessa reopened — not in a building, but outside, in the parking area of the old Uniway Bibles Church. With nothing but determination, faith, and the support of her community, she continued cooking, serving, and rebuilding from scratch.

Today, Ayessa Sue’s Foodie’s & Goodies stands stronger than ever. From daily meals to food trays, whole lechon, lechon packages, and full catering services, the business continues to grow while staying true to its heart: providing food made with passion, quality, and genuine care.

From a garage startup to a resilient community favorite, the story of Ayessa Sue’s Foodie’s & Goodies is proof that with hard work and heart, even the smallest beginnings can rise into something extraordinary.

How the Philippine Government
Helped the Business

The growth and recovery of Ayessa Sue’s Foodies & Goodies were supported not only by determination and community, but also by programs and assistance provided by the Philippine government, especially during challenging times.

During the pandemic and after Typhoon Odette, government agencies played a vital role in helping small businesses survive. Financial assistance programs, livelihood aid, and recovery initiatives helped ease the burden of losses and allowed entrepreneurs like Ayessa Sue to continue operating despite limited resources.

Local government units (LGUs) provided guidance through business permits, safety regulations, and compliance requirements, making it possible for the business to legally operate and rebuild with confidence. These processes ensured customer safety while supporting small entrepreneurs.

Government agencies such as the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) also contributed by offering training, seminars, and support programs focused on entrepreneurship, product improvement, and business sustainability. These opportunities helped strengthen management skills and improve service quality.

Through these combined efforts, the Philippine government helped create an environment where small businesses like Ayessa Sue’s Foodies & Goodies could recover, grow, and continue serving the community. This support became an important part of the business’s journey toward resilience and long-term success.
